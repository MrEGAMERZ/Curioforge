## Problem Statement

Primary school students (ages 7–12) are curious, bored, and creative — but they have nowhere
safe and personalized to take that energy. YouTube gives them passive content. Google gives them
adult-level text. Generic AI chatbots give walls of jargon that assume reading fluency.

No product today:
- Accepts a primary kid's spoken idea and runs with it
- Designs a *fun*, safe, age-appropriate activity from whatever materials they have at home
- Teaches the STEM concept behind the activity without it feeling like school
- Adapts vocabulary to a 7-year-old vs a 12-year-old in real time

## What Makes This Cool

A kid is bored on a Saturday afternoon. They pick up the app and say:
> "I want to make something that explodes!"

The AI doesn't panic or refuse. It asks:
> "Ooh! Do you have baking soda and vinegar at home? Because I know exactly how to make a safe
> volcano that REALLY erupts. Want to find out why it bubbles so much? Let's go."

**The "whoa" for judges:** voice in → personalized activity out → kid learns real science
without knowing they're learning.

## Target User

**Primary:** Age 7–12 (Grades 2–6), curious kids with limited adult supervision, shared family devices.
**Secondary:** Parents and teachers who want safe, educational screen time.

## Premises

1. **Voice is the right primary input** — primary kids speak faster and more naturally than they type. ✅
2. **Image input is the key differentiator** — "What do you have at home?" answered by a photo is
   10x faster than a typed list. ✅
3. **Fun IS the product** — competing on "educational AI" is crowded; "bored-kid companion that makes
   science happen" is not. ✅
4. **Safety is invisible** — age-appropriate guardrails baked into prompts; kids never see a refusal,
   only a redirect to something equally exciting. ✅

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Web Speech API + Gemini Vision (Two calls)
Browser `SpeechRecognition` transcribes voice → Gemini text model; image sent separately.
- Pro: simple, no audio API cost
- Con: English-only, choppy, two-step feel
- Effort: ~3h

### Approach B: Gemini 2.0 Flash Multimodal — Recommended
Single Gemini call accepts voice audio blob + image together.
- Pro: one model, one API, true multimodal, demo-worthy, extends to real-time
- Con: need to verify audio `inlineData` on free tier (D1-voice)
- Effort: ~4–5h but produces the visually impressive demo

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — Gemini 2.0 Flash Multimodal**, with Web Speech API as a live fallback.

The judges' "whoa" comes from voice + image → personalized activity in one shot. If Gemini audio
input is blocked on free tier, fall back gracefully to Web Speech → Gemini Vision + text (still
live, still impressive). Document outcome in ARCHITECTURE.md at Phase 0.

## Core User Flow (MVP)

```
Kid presses big mic button → speaks their idea
  (Optional) taps camera → photos materials on the table
Gemini receives audio + image → identifies idea + available materials
AI asks 1–2 follow-up questions (displayed + spoken via browser TTS)
Generates personalized activity plan:
  • What you'll make  • Why it's cool (STEM concept)
  • Step-by-step (using only what they photographed)
  • Safety notes (invisible guardrails)
  • "What do you think will happen?" (predict-first hook)
Kid does the activity → returns → AI explains results
```

## Success Criteria (Demo, ≤5 min)

1. Kid speaks an idea → AI responds with a named, personalized activity
2. Photo of materials → activity plan uses only those materials
3. Activity plan has what/why/how sections, age-appropriate language
4. No hardcoded responses; all content is live Gemini output
5. Errors are kid-friendly ("Hmm, let me think again!")
